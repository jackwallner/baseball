# Critical App Store Review — StatScout Warning Signs

> Reviewer lens: Guidelines 4.1 (Spam), 5.1.1/5.1.2 (Data / Payments), 5.2.3 (IP / Affiliate)
> Date: May 13, 2026

---

## 1. PREMIUM FEATURES & IAP — High Risk

### 1a. Privacy Policy Mismatch with Actual Products
**Severity: High**

- `PRIVACY.md` (line 17) states: *"StatScout offers an optional **one-time Pro Unlock** purchase."*
- The app actually sells **three** products: Monthly subscription, Yearly subscription (with free trial), and Lifetime purchase.
- `APP_STORE_SUBMISSION.md` and `StoreService.swift` confirm subscription offerings.
- **Guideline 5.1.1 violation**: Privacy disclosures must be accurate. A privacy policy that omits subscriptions and auto-renewal terms is misleading.
- `docs/privacy-policy.html` at least mentions "subscription options" but `PRIVACY.md` (which may be the canonical version) does not.

### 1b. Missing Terms of Service / EULA
**Severity: High**

- Auto-renewing subscriptions (Monthly + Yearly) require a **Terms of Service** or **EULA** that explains:
  - Billing terms
  - Auto-renewal and cancellation policy
  - Free trial conversion terms
- No TOS/EULA exists anywhere in the repo, on the support site, or in the app.
- Apple routinely rejects subscription apps that lack accessible subscription terms.

### 1c. Dead / Conflicting Purchase Code
**Severity: Medium**

- `StatScout/Services/StoreManager.swift` is an entire StoreKit-based purchase manager that appears to be **unused** (the app uses RevenueCat via `StoreService`).
- It still initializes a `Task.detached` transaction listener on launch (`listenForTransactions`) that could interfere with RevenueCat's own StoreKit handling.
- **Risk**: Two entitlement systems running simultaneously can cause purchase failures, duplicate charges, or false negatives in review testing.

### 1d. Overly Broad Entitlement Check
**Severity: Medium**

```swift
// StoreService.swift:108-110
extension CustomerInfo {
    var hasProEntitlement: Bool {
        !entitlements.active.isEmpty
    }
}
```
- This returns `true` if **any** entitlement is active. If RevenueCat ever adds a non-Pro entitlement (e.g., a promotional trial), the app incorrectly unlocks Pro.
- Should gate on the specific `StatScout Pro` / `pro` entitlement keys explicitly.

### 1e. Hardcoded RevenueCat API Key
**Severity: Low**

```swift
// StoreService.swift:12-13
enum RevenueCatConfig {
    static let apiKey = "appl_qNlZGCCfGWBTsvcxlqYSNCtRupx"
}
```
- While RevenueCat public keys are meant to be embeddable, hardcoding in source means anyone can extract and impersonate the app. Consider obfuscation or build-config injection.

## 2. BUILDING ON THE BACK OF MLB — Critical Risk

### 2a. Core Value Proposition Is Repackaged MLB Content
**Severity: Critical**

> **Note:** The app is properly branded as **"StatScout"** throughout. It does **not** call itself "Statcast" or "Savant" in the app name, icon, or store listing. This is a positive fact — there is no impersonation of an MLB product. The risks below stem from the data pipeline and marketing keywords, not from the app's branding.

- The app is a **thin wrapper** around Baseball Savant / MLB Statcast percentile data.
- `README.md` literally describes it as: *"Baseball Savant-style player percentile pages."*
- The backend (`backend/ingest.py`) scrapes Baseball Savant data via `pybaseball`.
- **Guideline 4.1 (Spam)**: Apps that primarily repackage someone else's website or content without adding substantial value are likely to be rejected.
- **Guideline 5.2.3**: Apps that suggest affiliation or endorsement by MLB will be rejected. While disclaimers exist, the app's entire value proposition is derivative of MLB's data product.

### 2b. Hotlinking MLB Assets
**Severity: High**

- Player headshots are loaded from `midfield.mlbstatic.com` (`APP_STORE_SUBMISSION.md` line 155, `Player.swift` headshot logic).
- Team colors use `MLBTeamColor.color(abbr)` — direct use of MLB team brand identity.
- Team abbreviations, team names, and player names are displayed throughout.
- The app deep-links to `baseballsavant.mlb.com` for every player (`PlayerProfileView.swift:97-99`) and every team (`TeamView.swift:164-166`).
- Deep-linking to the source you're scraping is strong evidence the app is a derivative repackaging.

### 2c. Keyword Squatting on MLB / Savant Brands
**Severity: Medium**

```
// APP_STORE_SUBMISSION.md:90
baseball,mlb,stats,analytics,savant,statcast,fantasy,metrics,...
```
- Keywords include `mlb`, `savant`, and `statcast` — trademarked terms owned by MLB Advanced Media.
- Apple's metadata reviewers flag unauthorized use of third-party trademarks in keywords.

### 2d. Marketing Site Claims "Statcast" as Data Source
**Severity: Medium**

- `docs/baseball-savvy.html` (line 521-523) displays: *"Data Source: Statcast — Public pitch-tracking data."*
- Statcast is an MLB proprietary data product. Calling it "public" is legally questionable. While the leaderboards are publicly displayed, the underlying data is not public domain.
- The "publicly available" framing may not satisfy Apple if MLB complains.

## 3. PRIVACY & DATA ACCURACY — Medium Risk

### 3a. Privacy Manifest Under-Reports Data Collection
**Severity: Medium**

- `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` declares **zero** collected data types (`NSPrivacyCollectedDataTypes` is an empty array).
- However, RevenueCat (a third-party SDK) collects:
  - Device identifiers
  - Purchase history
  - App interactions
- The manifest should declare `Purchase History` and `User ID` if RevenueCat is configured to collect them, or at minimum disclose the SDK's data practices.
- **Guideline 5.1.1**: Inaccurate privacy manifest answers can result in rejection.

### 3b. Mismatched Privacy Policy Dates
**Severity: Low**

- `PRIVACY.md` says "Last Updated: May 6, 2026"
- `docs/privacy-policy.html` says "Last updated: May 12, 2026"
- Inconsistent dates look sloppy to reviewers.

## 4. UX / FUNCTIONALITY ISSUES — Low-to-Medium Risk

### 4a. Dead Paywall Code in TeamView
**Severity: Low**

- `TeamView.swift` declares `@State private var showingPaywall = false` and a `.sheet(isPresented: $showingPaywall)` (line 85-87).
- Nothing in `TeamView` ever sets `showingPaywall = true`. The `.teamView` trigger exists in `PaywallTrigger` but is unused in this view.
- The actual past-season gating happens in `TeamsView.swift` (season menu) and `PlayerProfileView.swift` (season menu). Not a rejection reason, but indicates sloppy code review.

### 4b. Free Trial Disclosure
**Severity: Low**

- The yearly subscription includes a free trial (`APP_STORE_SUBMISSION.md` line 6, 78).
- The app uses `RevenueCatUI.PaywallView`, which generally handles trial disclosure, but the app's **custom paywall header copy** (`PaywallView.swift:27-55`) does not mention the free trial in any trigger subtitle.
- If RevenueCat's default UI is suppressed or fails to load, the user might purchase without clear trial context.

## Bottom Line

As a critical reviewer, the two biggest red flags are:

1. **The app is a thin client for MLB's proprietary Statcast/Savant data.** It scrapes the data, repackages it with minimal visual chrome, and hotlinks MLB assets. This is textbook "repackaging someone else's content" (Guideline 4.1). The MLB disclaimers help, but they don't change the fundamental lack of original value.

2. **The privacy policy and legal disclosures do not match the actual subscription products.** Offering auto-renewing subscriptions without a TOS and with an inaccurate privacy policy is a near-certain rejection under Guideline 5.1.1.

**Recommendation**: Do **not** submit to App Store review until the High/Critical items are addressed. The MLB content-risk issue in particular may require legal review or a pivot to a more transformative data presentation.

## 5. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE — How Other Apps Handle MLB Data

### 5a. The Legal Reality: MLB's Copyright Notice Is Explicit

MLB's official copyright notice (`http://gdx.mlb.com/components/copyright.txt`):

> "The accounts, descriptions, data and presentation in the referring page (the 'Materials') are proprietary content of MLB Advanced Media, L.P ('MLBAM'). **Only individual, non-commercial, non-bulk use of the Materials is permitted and any other use of the Materials is prohibited without prior written authorization from MLBAM.**"

**This directly prohibits what StatScout does:**
- Scrapes Savant data nightly via `pybaseball` (bulk extraction)
- Stores it in Supabase (redistribution)
- Sells premium access to it (commercial use)
- All without MLBAM written authorization

### 5b. MLB Actively Enforces Against App Developers

**Case 1: Bulbous Ventures "Baseball" App (2008)**
- A free iPhone stats app received a **cease-and-desist** from MLB Advanced Media for using team logos.
- MLB's spokesperson later clarified: *player stats and names are in the public domain*, but the **use of those stats and names in conjunction with team logos and other images** created trademark infringement.
- StatScout uses team colors (`MLBTeamColor.color(abbr)`), team abbreviations in branded circles, and hotlinks MLB headshot assets — all of which fall under the same category of protected presentation.

**Case 2: CBC Distribution v. MLBAM (2005-2006)**
- MLB sued a fantasy baseball provider for using player names and stats without a license.
- **CBC won.** The court ruled that player names and statistics are **facts in the public domain** and cannot be copyrighted.
- **Key distinction for StatScout:** CBC was using raw stats. StatScout is using Statcast percentile rankings, which are MLB's **computed derivative metrics** (xwOBA, Barrel%, etc.) presented in MLB's percentile format. These are not raw facts — they are MLB's proprietary analytical product.

**Case 3: STA v. MLBAM (2014)**
- Sports Technology Applications entered into an **official license agreement** with MLBAM to use MLB intellectual property in an app.
- This proves MLB **does license IP to app developers**, but it requires a formal agreement, fees, and MLB oversight.
- StatScout has no such agreement.

### 5c. How Competing Apps Actually Survive

| App | Strategy | Data Source | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FanGraphs App** | Official app from an established stats site with its own brand and data calculations | FanGraphs' own proprietary metrics (WAR, wRC+, etc.) | **Low** — they own their data |
| **Diamond: Baseball Stats** | Uses **college baseball** data, not MLB | D1Baseball, AP Poll, RPI | **Low** — college data has different ownership |
| **Superfan Sports: MLB** | Live scores & odds app | Likely uses a **licensed data provider** (e.g., SportsDataIO, Sportradar) | **Low** — pays for data license |
| **Baseball Stats Tracker Touch** | User manually enters their own stats | No third-party data at all | **None** |
| **MLB At Bat** | Official MLB app | MLBAM owns everything | **None** |

### 5d. What StatScout Gets Wrong That Others Get Right

**1. No Original Computation**
- FanGraphs doesn't just display MLB stats — it calculates **proprietary metrics** (WAR, FIP, wRC+) that fans can't get elsewhere.
- StatScout computes nothing. It copies MLB's percentile rankings verbatim. This is the definition of repackaging.

**2. Using MLB's Brand Identity**
- Superfan Sports avoids team logos and likely uses generic colors or licensed imagery.
- StatScout uses exact MLB team colors (`MLBTeamColor.color(abbr)`), exact abbreviations in branded circles, and hotlinks `midfield.mlbstatic.com` headshots. The Macworld case shows this exact combination (stats + logos + team identity) triggers MLB enforcement.

**3. Deep-Linking to the Source**
- Every player profile has a Safari link to `baseballsavant.mlb.com`.
- Every team view has a Safari link to `baseballsavant.mlb.com/team/[abbr]`.
- This is strong evidence the app is a derivative wrapper. No competitor that isn't officially affiliated deep-links to MLB's own product pages.

**4. Marketing Language**
- StatScout's marketing site (`docs/baseball-savvy.html`) explicitly names "Statcast" as the data source and uses phrases like "Baseball Analytics at a Glance" that mirror MLB's own branding.
- Successful third-party apps (Superfan, Diamond) never name their upstream data source in marketing. They brand their own experience.

### 5e. The Licensing Path That Exists (But Is Costly)

- **SportsDataIO** and **Sportradar** offer licensed MLB data APIs for commercial use. Pricing is enterprise-level (typically $5,000-$50,000+/year depending on scope).
- The MLB Stats API (`statsapi.mlb.com`) is explicitly for **individual, non-commercial use** only.
- StatScout's `pybaseball` → Baseball Savant pipeline is scraping the Savant web interface, which is even more restricted than the Stats API.

### 5f. How StatScout Could Pivot

Based on what actually works in the market:

| Approach | Feasibility | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Switch to licensed data provider** (SportsDataIO, Sportradar) | High cost | Would solve legal risk, but likely unprofitable for a small indie app |
| **Calculate own metrics from raw play-by-play** | High dev cost | Would require accessing raw pitch-level data and computing percentiles independently — this is what FanGraphs does |
| **Focus on non-MLB baseball** (college, minors, international) | Medium | Diamond proves college baseball apps can succeed; data is cheaper |
| **User-generated / fantasy tools** | Medium | Baseball Stats Tracker Touch model — user enters their own league data |
| **Transformative visualization + fair use commentary** | Risky | Could argue the percentile bars and mobile layout are transformative, but the underlying data is still MLB's derivative product |
| **Remove all MLB branding** (team colors, logos, headshots, deep links) and use only player names + raw stats | Medium | Reduces trademark risk, but the core data scraping issue remains |

### 5g. What Other Apps Do With Premium Features

Looking at competitors that charge:

- **Diamond**: Offers premium for historical data and advanced filtering, but it's a college app with a clear value-add (live scores + rankings + draft prospects). No ambiguity about what you pay for.
- **FanGraphs**: Free app with ads; premium is the FanGraphs membership (article access, RosterResource tools, ad-free). Their premium is for **content and tools they created**, not for access to someone else's data.
- **Baseball Stats Tracker Touch**: One-time purchase for stat tracking features (export, projections). Entirely self-contained.

**StatScout's premium model is unusual:** it gates access to **historical copies of scraped MLB data** and **comparison views of scraped MLB data**. No competitor charges for access to historical MLB stats without a formal data license.

## 6. UPDATED SUMMARY

The competitive landscape research makes the MLB risk **worse**, not better:

1. **MLB's copyright notice explicitly prohibits commercial, bulk, and redistributed use** — all three of which StatScout does.
2. **MLB has a documented history of sending cease-and-desist letters to iPhone app developers** for using team logos and brand identity in conjunction with stats.
3. **The player-names-are-public ruling (CBC v MLBAM) does not apply** because Statcast percentiles are MLB's computed derivative metrics, not raw factual statistics.
4. **Every comparable app that survives either**: (a) has its own data (FanGraphs), (b) pays for a license (Superfan), (c) uses non-MLB data (Diamond), or (d) avoids all MLB branding and third-party data entirely (Tracker Touch).
5. **StatScout does none of these.** It is the exact profile of apps that get shut down.

**The path to App Store approval requires either**: obtaining an MLBAM data license (expensive), pivoting to college/independent league data, or adding substantial original computation so the app is no longer a derivative repackaging of Savant content.
